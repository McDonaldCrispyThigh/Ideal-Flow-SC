\begin{abstract}
This paper applies the Schwarz--Christoffel (SC) conformal mapping to simulate
steady, irrotational, incompressible ideal fluid flow inside a 12-vertex
simplification of the Boulder County, Colorado boundary polygon. The SC
transformation maps the upper half-plane $\HH$ conformally onto the polygonal
domain $\Omega$, converting analytically tractable complex potentials in $\HH$
into streamline and equipotential patterns in the physical domain. Two flow
models are derived and implemented: a uniform ideal flow $W = U\zeta$
providing the baseline conformal grid, and a doubly-connected flow in which a
downtown obstacle is modelled via the Milne-Thomson circle theorem. The SC
parameter problem is solved by softmax pre-vertex parameterisation combined
with Levenberg--Marquardt least squares on side-length ratios computed by
Gauss--Legendre quadrature. All results are produced through the forward SC
map, yielding streamlines that satisfy the no-penetration boundary condition
exactly by construction. A closed-form verification on the rectangle-to-half-plane
map confirms the implementation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Computing fluid flow in an arbitrary domain is a hard problem: Laplace's
equation $\nabla^2 \phi = 0$ admits no elementary closed-form solution in an
irregular geometry. The classical approach uses conformal mapping. A conformal
map $z = f(\zeta)$ from a canonical domain (the unit disk or the upper
half-plane) to the physical region $\Omega$ transforms the Laplace equation
from one domain to the other while preserving harmonicity, so that any
analytic function $W(\zeta)$ satisfying the required boundary conditions in
the canonical domain produces a valid solution in $\Omega$ via
$\tilde{W}(z) = W(f^{-1}(z))$.

The Schwarz--Christoffel (SC) transformation, developed independently by
H.~A.~Schwarz and E.~B.~Christoffel in the 1860s, gives an explicit integral
formula for the conformal map from the upper half-plane $\HH$ to any simply
connected polygon. For analytic polygons the parameter problem (choosing
pre-images of the vertices on the real axis) can sometimes be solved in
closed form; for general $n$-gons it must be treated numerically. The modern
numerical theory was systematised by Driscoll and Trefethen~\citep{DT02}; the
classical theory is in Nehari~\citep{Nehari52}, Ahlfors~\citep{Ahlfors79},
and Walker~\citep{Walker64}.

We apply the SC transformation to a 12-vertex polygon derived from the
Boulder County, Colorado boundary, chosen for its mix of convex and reflex
vertices as a non-trivial test of the numerical pipeline. Two flow regimes
are considered: (i)~uniform ideal flow, providing the baseline conformal grid,
and (ii)~doubly-connected flow with an interior obstacle modelled via the
Milne-Thomson circle theorem. The polygon's geometric complexity exercises
the full pipeline; any physical interpretation as "airflow over a city" is
illustrative. The mathematical framework is drawn from
\citet{AF03,DT02,Nehari52}; the fluid-dynamics background from \citet{BO99}
and \citet{MT68}.

A closed-form verification against the rectangle-to-half-plane map (an
elliptic-integral identity) is included to certify the numerical pipeline
independently of the Boulder application.

\paragraph{Organisation.}
Section~\ref{sec:theory} develops the SC mapping theory and complex-potential
framework, with step-by-step derivations of every key result.
Section~\ref{sec:numerical} describes the numerical algorithms and verifies

\section{Mathematical Framework}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Ideal Fluid Flow and the Complex Potential}
\label{sec:ideal-flow}

A steady two-dimensional flow is \emph{ideal} when the velocity field
$\mathbf{u} = (u, v)$ is simultaneously irrotational
($\partial_x v - \partial_y u = 0$) and incompressible
($\partial_x u + \partial_y v = 0$). Irrotationality in a simply connected
region forces $\mathbf{u}$ to be a gradient field $\mathbf{u} = \nabla \phi$
(velocity potential), and incompressibility then gives $\nabla^2 \phi = 0$.
Incompressibility independently forces $\mathbf{u}$ to be the skew-gradient of
a stream function $\psi$ via $u = \partial_y \psi$, $v = -\partial_x \psi$, and
irrotationality then gives $\nabla^2 \psi = 0$. Comparing the two
representations produces the Cauchy--Riemann equations
\begin{equation}
  \partial_x \phi = \partial_y \psi,
  \qquad
  \partial_y \phi = -\partial_x \psi,
  \label{eq:CR}
\end{equation}
so the \emph{complex potential}
\begin{equation}
  W(z) = \phi(x, y) + i\,\psi(x, y), \qquad z = x + iy,
  \label{eq:W}
\end{equation}
is analytic on the flow domain.

\paragraph{Velocity from $W'(z)$.}
For any analytic function the complex derivative can be evaluated along the
real direction: $W'(z) = \partial_x W = \partial_x \phi + i\, \partial_x \psi$.
Using $\partial_x \phi = u$ and the Cauchy--Riemann equation
$\partial_x \psi = -\partial_y \phi = -v$, we obtain
\begin{equation}
  W'(z) = u - i v = \conj{u + iv},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{u} = \conj{W'(z)}.
  \label{eq:velocity}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Physical interpretation.}
Level curves of $\phi$ are \emph{equipotentials}; level curves of $\psi$ are
\emph{streamlines}. The Cauchy--Riemann equations imply
$\nabla \phi \cdot \nabla \psi = 0$, so streamlines and equipotentials meet
orthogonally wherever $W'(z) \neq 0$. The no-penetration condition on a solid
boundary $\partial \Omega$ reads $\mathbf{u} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}} = 0$,
equivalent to $\psi = \mathrm{const}$ along $\partial \Omega$.

\subsection{Conformal Invariance of Harmonicity}
\label{sec:conformal}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:conformal}
Let $f : \HH \to \Omega$ be a bijective analytic map with $f'(\zeta) \neq 0$.
If $W(\zeta)$ is analytic on $\HH$, then $\tilde{W}(z) = W(f^{-1}(z))$ is
analytic on $\Omega$; in particular, both $\tilde{\phi} = \Real \tilde{W}$
and $\tilde{\psi} = \Imag \tilde{W}$ satisfy Laplace's equation on $\Omega$.
\end{lemma}

This is the structural fact that makes conformal mapping work for ideal flow.
Any solution of Laplace's equation in $\HH$ pulls back to a solution in
$\Omega$, and the no-penetration condition $\psi = 0$ on $\partial \HH$
(easy to arrange by the method of images) becomes $\psi = 0$ on
$\partial \Omega$ automatically.

\subsection{The Riemann Mapping Theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Riemann, 1851]
\label{thm:riemann}
Let $\Omega \subsetneq \CC$ be a simply connected domain. There exists a
bijective conformal map $f : \HH \to \Omega$, unique up to post-composition
with a real M\"obius transformation of $\HH$.
\end{theorem}

A proof is in~\citet[Ch.~6]{Ahlfors79}. The Boulder polygon is simply connected, so $f$ exists. The three real
parameters of the M\"obius freedom correspond to fixing three real pre-images
on $\partial \HH = \RR \cup \{\infty\}$.

\subsection{The Schwarz--Christoffel Formula}
\label{sec:sc-formula}

For a polygon $\Omega$ with vertices $w_1, \dots, w_n$ (counter-clockwise) and
interior angles $\alpha_k \pi$, $\alpha_k \in (0, 2)$, the SC map takes the
form~\citep[Ch.~V, \S6]{Nehari52}\citep[Ch.~6, \S2.2]{Ahlfors79}\citep[Ch.~2]{DT02}
\begin{equation}
  f(\zeta) = A + C \int_{\zeta_0}^{\zeta}
    \prod_{k=1}^{n} (t - \zeta_k)^{\alpha_k - 1} \dif t,
  \label{eq:SC}
\end{equation}
where $\zeta_k \in \RR$ are the \emph{pre-vertices}, $A \in \CC$ is a
translation, and $C \in \CC$ encodes scale and rotation. The angle-sum
identity $\sum_k \alpha_k = n - 2$ follows from the total CCW exterior turning
$= 2\pi$, and convergence at infinity (when $\zeta_n = \infty$) holds whenever
$\alpha_n > 0$.

The structural argument that produces~\eqref{eq:SC} computes $\arg f'(t)$ for
real $t \in (\zeta_k, \zeta_{k+1})$. Factors $(t - \zeta_j)^{\alpha_j - 1}$
with $j \leq k$ contribute zero phase; those with $j > k$ contribute
$(\alpha_j - 1)\pi$ on the principal branch. Therefore
\begin{equation}
  \arg f'(t) = \arg C + \pi \sum_{j = k+1}^{n} (\alpha_j - 1)
  \label{eq:argfprime}
\end{equation}
is constant on each open interval $(\zeta_k, \zeta_{k+1})$, so its image is a
straight segment. As $t$ crosses $\zeta_k$, the tangent turns CCW by
$(1 - \alpha_k)\pi$, reproducing the prescribed exterior angle and hence the
interior angle $\alpha_k\pi$.

\paragraph{Interior-angle formula in practice.}
Given vertices $w_0, \dots, w_{n-1}$ (CCW), the signed exterior turning at
$w_k$ is $\arg\!\bigl((w_{k+1} - w_k) / (w_k - w_{k-1})\bigr)$, so
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_k = 1 - \frac{1}{\pi}\,
    \arg\!\left( \frac{w_{k+1} - w_k}{w_k - w_{k-1}} \right),
  \label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
with indices modulo $n$.

\subsection{M\"obius Normalisation and the Parameter Problem}

The real M\"obius group $\mathrm{PSL}(2, \RR)$, acting on $\HH$ via
$\zeta \mapsto (a\zeta + b)/(c\zeta + d)$, has three real parameters. Three of
the $n$ pre-vertices can therefore be fixed at prescribed values without loss
of generality. We use
\begin{equation}
  \zeta_0 = -1, \qquad \zeta_1 = 0, \qquad \zeta_{n-1} = 1,
  \label{eq:normalisation}
\end{equation}
leaving $n - 3$ free parameters $\zeta_2 < \cdots < \zeta_{n-2}$ in $(0, 1)$.
For Boulder County, $n = 12$ yields $n - 3 = 9$ free pre-vertices. These are
determined by matching side-length ratios; see Section~\ref{sec:param}.

\subsection{Method of Images}
\label{sec:images}

Any analytic $W(\zeta)$ with $\Imag W = 0$ on $\RR$ automatically satisfies
$\psi = 0$ on $\partial \Omega$ after pull-back via $f$. To add a singularity
at $s = a + ib \in \HH$ (with $b > 0$) while preserving $\Imag W = 0$ on the
real axis, we pair it with a mirror-image singularity at $\bar{s} = a - ib$.
For a source of strength $Q > 0$ the image-pair potential is
\begin{equation}
  W_{\mathrm{source}}(\zeta)
  = \frac{Q}{2\pi} \bigl[ \log(\zeta - s) + \log(\zeta - \bar{s}) \bigr].
  \label{eq:source-pair}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Algebraic verification.}
Take $\zeta = x \in \RR$:
\begin{align*}
  \log(x - s) + \log(x - \bar{s})
  &= \log\!\bigl[(x - a)^2 + b^2\bigr]
     + i\,\bigl[\arg(x - a - ib) + \arg(x - a + ib)\bigr].
\end{align*}
The numbers $x - a - ib$ and $x - a + ib$ are complex conjugates, so their
arguments are opposite in sign, making the bracketed imaginary part vanish.
Therefore
\begin{equation}
  \Imag\!\bigl[\log(x - s) + \log(x - \bar{s})\bigr] = 0
  \quad \text{for all } x \in \RR.
  \label{eq:imsum}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Milne-Thomson Circle Theorem}
\label{sec:MT}

\begin{theorem}[Milne-Thomson, 1940]
\label{thm:MT}
Let $W_0(\zeta)$ be analytic on $\CC \setminus D$ where
$D = \{\zeta : \abs{\zeta - \zeta_0} \leq a\}$, with no singularities on
$\partial D$. Then
\begin{equation}
  W^{\mathrm{ext}}(\zeta) = W_0(\zeta)
  + \conj{W_0\!\left(\zeta_0 + \frac{a^2}{\conj{\zeta - \zeta_0}}\right)}
  \label{eq:MT}
\end{equation}
is analytic on the exterior of $D$ with the same singularities as $W_0$, and
satisfies $\Imag W^{\mathrm{ext}} = 0$ on $\partial D$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Writing $\zeta - \zeta_0 = a e^{i\theta}$ on $\partial D$ gives
$a^2/\conj{\zeta - \zeta_0} = \zeta - \zeta_0$, so the inversion argument
in~\eqref{eq:MT} collapses to $\zeta$ itself and
$W^{\mathrm{ext}}|_{\partial D} = W_0(\zeta) + \conj{W_0(\zeta)} = 2\Real W_0(\zeta) \in \RR$.
Analyticity of the second term in $\zeta$ on $\CC \setminus \overline{D}$
follows from the Schwarz reflection principle for
circles~\citep[Ch.~4, \S6.5]{Ahlfors79}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Upper-half-plane specialisation.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:MT} enforces $\partial D$ as a streamline on
$\CC \setminus D$. To additionally satisfy $\psi = 0$ on $\partial \HH$, the
method of images requires reflecting the obstacle dipole to $\conj{\zeta_0}$.
For $W_0 = U\zeta$ the combined expression
\begin{equation}
  W_{\mathrm{urban}}(\zeta) = U \zeta
    + \frac{U a^2}{\zeta - \zeta_0}
    + \frac{U a^2}{\zeta - \conj{\zeta_0}}
  \label{eq:urban}
\end{equation}
is holomorphic in $\zeta$. The image dipole at $\conj{\zeta_0}$ enforces
$\psi = 0$ on $\partial \HH$ exactly (conjugate-pair cancellation as in
Section~\ref{sec:images}); the dipole at $\zeta_0$ approximates no-penetration
on $\partial D$ to leading order in $(a/\Imag\,\zeta_0)^2$
\citep[\S6.21]{MT68}, an order-of-magnitude estimate rather than a rigorous
bound. Figure~\ref{fig:mt-schematic} illustrates the construction.

\subsection{SC Crowding and the Conformal Modulus}
\label{sec:crowding}

A practical obstruction to numerical SC is \emph{crowding}: for polygons with
strongly different edge-length scales, pre-vertices cluster exponentially on
the real axis. The phenomenon reflects a rigid complex-analytic invariant.
For a rectangle of width $2K(k)$ and height $K'(k)$, with $K, K'$ the complete
elliptic integrals~\citep[\S19.2]{NIST10}, the conformal modulus
$m(R) = 2K(k)/K'(k)$ is a conformal invariant. The asymptotic expansion
of $K(k)$~\citep[\S19.12]{NIST10} gives inner pre-vertex spacing
\begin{equation}
  1/k - 1 \;\sim\; 8 e^{-\pi m(R) / 2}
  \qquad \text{as } m(R) \to \infty.
  \label{eq:crowd-asym}
\end{equation}

For a general polygon containing a long thin region of aspect ratio $L$,
spacing across that region contracts exponentially in
$L$~\citep[Ch.~2]{DT02}. Figure~\ref{fig:crowding} shows pre-vertex spacings
recovered by our solver tracking the theoretical curve until the $O(N^{-1})$
Gauss--Legendre branch-point error dominates ($\sim 10^{-3}$ to $10^{-4}$).
Spacing falls below $10^{-8}$ at $L \approx 7$ and beyond $L \gtrsim 10$
approaches double precision, limiting practical SC solvers to $n \lesssim 20$
vertices with mild aspect ratios. The 2752-vertex Boulder boundary must
therefore be simplified to 12 vertices (Section~\ref{sec:preprocess}) with
all interior angles in $[0.35\pi, 1.75\pi]$.

\section{Numerical Implementation}
\label{sec:numerical}

\subsection{Polygon Preprocessing}
\label{sec:preprocess}

The raw Boulder County TIGER/Line boundary~\citep{TIGER25} has 2752 vertices.
Douglas--Peucker simplification with an adaptive binary-search tolerance
reduces it to 14 vertices; two vertices with excessively acute angles below
$0.35\pi$ are then removed iteratively by merging adjacent edges, yielding the
final $n = 12$ polygon. The polygon is normalised to $O(1)$ scale and centred
at the origin for numerical stability. The angle bounds $[0.35\pi, 1.75\pi]$
are chosen from the crowding discussion of Section~\ref{sec:crowding}.

\subsection{The SC Parameter Problem}
\label{sec:param}

The $n - 3 = 9$ free pre-vertices $\zeta_2, \dots, \zeta_{n-2} \in (0, 1)$
are chosen so that the image polygon $f(\RR)$ has the correct side-length
ratios. Define the $k$-th side length
\begin{equation}
  L_k = \abs{\, \int_{\zeta_k}^{\zeta_{k+1}}
    \prod_{j=1}^{n} (t - \zeta_j)^{\alpha_j - 1} \dif t \,},
\end{equation}
and require
\begin{equation}
  \frac{L_k}{L_{n-1}}
  = \frac{\abs{w_{k+1} - w_k}}{\abs{w_0 - w_{n-1}}},
  \qquad k = 0, \dots, n - 2.
  \label{eq:param}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Softmax reparameterisation.}
Given $p \in \RR^{n-3}$, define weights and cumulative pre-vertex positions:
\begin{equation}
  w_j(p) = \frac{e^{p_j}}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-3} e^{p_i} + 1},
  \qquad
  \zeta_{k+1} = \sum_{j=1}^{k} w_j(p).
  \label{eq:softmax}
\end{equation}
Since $e^{p_j} > 0$, each $w_j > 0$ and the cumulative sums are strictly
increasing. Let $S = \sum_i e^{p_i}$; then $\sum_j w_j = S/(S + 1) < 1$, so
every $\zeta_k \in (0, 1)$. The ordering and box constraints are thus
automatic, and the optimisation over $p \in \RR^{n-3}$ is unconstrained.

\paragraph{Solver and quadrature.}
System~\eqref{eq:param} is solved by Levenberg--Marquardt least squares~\citep[Ch.~7]{SB02} via
SciPy's \texttt{least\_squares} routine. Each side-length integral is
evaluated by $N = 500$-node Gauss--Legendre quadrature~\citep[\S3.5(v)]{NIST10}\citep{DB08} on $[-1, 1]$ after
affine rescaling to $[\zeta_k, \zeta_{k+1}]$; nodes and weights are
pre-computed and reused across iterations, so each evaluation is a single
vectorised dot product. Levenberg--Marquardt update details are in
Appendix~\ref{app:lm}.

Figure~\ref{fig:prevertex-polygon} shows the recovered pre-vertex layout for
the 12-vertex Boulder County polygon alongside the polygon itself, with each
$\zeta_k \leftrightarrow w_k$ pair colour-matched. The concentration of
pre-vertices toward one side of the real axis directly visualises the
crowding phenomenon of Section~\ref{sec:crowding}: vertices on the narrow
western flank of the polygon share their pre-vertex region with several
close neighbours.

\paragraph{Determining $A$ and $C$.}
Once the pre-vertices are known, $A, C \in \CC$ are obtained from the
overdetermined linear system $A + C \cdot I_k = w_k$ for
$k = 0, 1, \dots, n - 1$, where $I_k$ is the SC integral from a reference
point $\zeta_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0.5i$ to $\zeta_k + \varepsilon$ (with a small
complex offset $\varepsilon$ to avoid the branch point). The least-squares
solution is computed via \texttt{numpy.linalg.lstsq}.

\subsection{Forward Map and Streamline Computation}
\label{sec:forward}

All flow results are produced via the \emph{forward-map} approach. A
pre-vertex-adapted grid of $\zeta$ values in $\HH$ (dense samples between
every adjacent pre-vertex pair, 60 logarithmically spaced imaginary parts)
is mapped forward to $z = f(\zeta)$, each SC integral evaluated by a
$250$-node Gauss--Legendre rule along an L-shaped path in $\HH$ that clears
the real-axis branch cuts (Figure~\ref{fig:branch-path}). The complex potential $W(\zeta)$ is evaluated
analytically at each $\zeta$ point. The resulting scattered pairs
$(z_i, W(\zeta_i))$ are interpolated onto a regular physical-plane grid
(\texttt{scipy.interpolate.griddata}, linear method), and contours of
$\Imag W$ (streamlines) and $\Real W$ (equipotentials) are extracted.

This avoids the per-pixel Newton iteration that inverse mapping would require.
Grid points from $\HH$ land strictly inside $\Omega$ by construction, so the
interpolated field is zero outside $\Omega$ with smooth contours at the
boundary.

\subsection{Verification on the Rectangle Map}
\label{sec:verify}

Before applying the pipeline to Boulder, we verify it against the one
polygonal case with a closed-form SC map: the rectangle. The classical
construction~\citep[Ch.~V, \S6]{Nehari52}\citep[Ch.~6, \S2.3]{Ahlfors79} uses the symmetric pre-vertex set
$\{-1/k, -1, 1, 1/k\}$ with elliptic modulus $0 < k < 1$, giving
\begin{equation}
  f(\zeta) = \int_0^\zeta
    \frac{\dif t}{\sqrt{(1 - t^2)(1 - k^2 t^2)}}
  = F(\arcsin \zeta,\, k),
  \label{eq:rect-ellip}
\end{equation}
with $F$ the incomplete elliptic integral of the first
kind~\citep[{\S}19.2]{NIST10}. The corner images are
$f(\pm 1) = \pm K(k)$ and $f(\pm 1/k) = \pm K(k) + i K'(k)$, so the image
rectangle has width $2K(k)$ and height $K'(k)$; its conformal modulus
matches Section~\ref{sec:crowding}.

\paragraph{Adaptation to the project's parameterisation.}
The softmax reparameterisation~\eqref{eq:softmax} constrains all free
pre-vertices to $(0, 1)$ and fixes three anchors at $\{-1, 0, 1\}$. Values
$\pm 1/k$ with $k < 1$ lie outside this range, so the classical symmetric
placement cannot be used directly. The two normalisations are related by the
unique real M\"obius transformation matching the ordered cross-ratio on the
real axis:
\begin{equation}
  [-1, 0; s, 1] = [-1/k, -1; 1, 1/k],
\end{equation}
where $[a, b; c, d] = (a - c)(b - d) / [(a - d)(b - c)]$. Computing both
sides,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1 + s}{2s} = \frac{(1 + k)^2}{4k}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  s(1 + k)^2 = 2k(1 + s)
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  s = \frac{2k}{1 + k^2}.
  \label{eq:s-of-k}
\end{equation}
This gives a one-to-one correspondence between $k \in (0, 1)$ in the classical
picture and the single free pre-vertex $s \in (0, 1)$ in our pipeline. For a
target rectangle of conformal modulus $m(R) = 2K(k) / K'(k)$, the pipeline
must recover the corresponding $s$ determined by~\eqref{eq:s-of-k}.

\paragraph{Numerical verification.}
Take $m(R) = 2$ (the standard $2 \times 1$ rectangle), giving
$k = 1/\sqrt{2}$ and $s_{\text{exact}} = 2\sqrt{2}/3 \approx 0.94281$. The
softmax~+~Levenberg--Marquardt solver converges to
$s_{\text{recovered}} = 0.94296$, with absolute error
$\approx 1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ on the pre-vertex and side-length residual
$\|r\|_2 \approx 7 \times 10^{-5}$. The accuracy floor reflects $O(N^{-1})$
Gauss--Legendre convergence at the integrable $(t - \zeta_k)^{-1/2}$
branch-point endpoints~\citep{DB08}; a Duffy substitution
$t - \zeta_k = u^2$ would absorb the singularity and recover near machine
precision at the cost of re-working the quadrature module. The current
$\sim 10^{-4}$ accuracy is sufficient for the qualitative Boulder results of
Section~\ref{sec:apply}. Figure~\ref{fig:verify} shows the configuration and
LM convergence history.

\subsection{Uniform Ideal Flow}
\label{sec:res-uniform}

\paragraph{Topological note.}
Uniform flow streamlines $\Imag\,\zeta = \eta_0$ are horizontal lines in
$\HH$ running from $-\infty$ to $+\infty$, which on the Riemann sphere meet
at a single point at infinity. The SC map sends that point to one fixed
vertex on $\partial\Omega$, so every streamline becomes a closed curve in
$\Omega$. The circulatory appearance of Figure~\ref{fig:uniform} is therefore
intrinsic to mapping $\HH$ onto a bounded polygon. To obtain crossing
streamlines, replace $W = U\zeta$ with a source-sink pair
$W = U\log[(\zeta - \zeta_{\mathrm{src}})/(\zeta - \zeta_{\mathrm{sink}})]$.

\paragraph{Uniform flow results.}
Figure~\ref{fig:uniform} presents the uniform flow $W = U\zeta$ in $\Omega$.
Streamlines $\psi = \mathrm{const}$ in panel~(a) stay inside $\partial \Omega$,
satisfying the no-penetration condition exactly by construction. Panel~(b)
overlays streamlines and equipotentials; their orthogonality throughout the
interior is the definitive visual confirmation of conformality, a consequence
of the Cauchy--Riemann equations~\eqref{eq:CR} wherever $f'(\zeta) \neq 0$.

\subsection{Doubly-Connected Flow with an Urban Obstacle}
\label{sec:res-urban}

The downtown commercial core (hardcoded downtown Boulder polygon,
$9.2$\,km$^2$, clipped inside the county boundary)
is mapped to $\HH$ via $f^{-1}$; its pre-image is enclosed by a minimum
enclosing circle (Welzl's algorithm~\citep{Welzl91}) of radius $a$ centred at
$\zeta_0 \in \HH$. The urban-obstacle potential~\eqref{eq:urban} then applies,
with achieved separation ratio $\Imag(\zeta_0)/a \approx 3.96$ for the
Boulder County run, giving a leading-order term of size
$O\bigl((a/\Imag\,\zeta_0)^2\bigr) \approx 6.4\%$.

Figure~\ref{fig:urban} shows the result. The Milne-Thomson dipole at
$\zeta_0$ enforces no-penetration on the enclosing circle; the image term
at $\conj{\zeta_0}$ restores $\psi = 0$ on $\RR$. Streamlines deflect
visibly around the purple obstacle, with local acceleration on its flanks.

\paragraph{Remark on modularity.}
Comparing Figures~\ref{fig:uniform}(a) and~\ref{fig:urban}, both panels
share the same SC map $f : \HH \to \Omega$ and the same normalised polygon,
so every visible difference comes from the analytic form of $W(\zeta)$ alone
(uniform flow vs.\ the urban-obstacle dipole~\eqref{eq:urban}). The SC map
is computed once; the physical model is switched by evaluating a different
closed-form expression in $\HH$.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

The Schwarz--Christoffel transformation provides an exact, closed-form
analytic framework for ideal fluid flow in a simply connected polygonal
domain. The forward-map approach evaluates analytically tractable potentials
in $\HH$ and maps the results to $\Omega$, yielding streamlines that satisfy
the no-penetration condition exactly by construction. Two complex potentials
were derived in detail and visualised on the Boulder polygon: uniform flow
and a doubly-connected model with a Milne-Thomson interior obstacle. A
closed-form verification on the rectangle-to-half-plane map (an
elliptic-integral identity) confirmed the numerical pipeline to within the
expected quadrature-limited accuracy ($\sim 10^{-4}$).

The principal mathematical content is the interplay between conformal-mapping
theory (Riemann Mapping Theorem, SC integral formula, interior-angle
structure, M\"obius normalisation, Schwarz reflection) and ideal-flow theory
(complex potential, method of images, Milne-Thomson circle theorem). The
SC formula~\eqref{eq:SC} encodes polygon geometry through the exponents
$\alpha_k - 1$: each vertex angle becomes a branch-point strength. The
Cauchy--Riemann equations, through the conformality of $f$, guarantee that
every solution of Laplace's equation in $\HH$ pulls back to a solution in
$\Omega$.

A practical obstruction identified in Section~\ref{sec:crowding} is
\emph{SC crowding}: the pre-vertex spacing across a long region of aspect
ratio $L$ contracts exponentially in $L$, a rigid conformal invariant tied
to the modulus of the region. This forces the 2752-vertex Boulder County boundary
to be simplified to 12 vertices before any SC computation is feasible.

\paragraph{Future directions.}
Two extensions are natural.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \textbf{Exact doubly-connected SC map.} The Milne-Thomson
  approximation~\eqref{eq:urban} introduces a small but non-zero
  approximation error (see Section~\ref{sec:res-urban}). An exact
  doubly-connected SC map~\citep[Ch.~4, \S4.9]{DT02}, implemented via the
  Schottky double or a circular slit canonical region, would eliminate
  this approximation and give a rigorous treatment.

\item \textbf{Multiply connected domains.} The formalism extends to domains
  with several obstacles via the Schottky-double conformal map, enabling an
  SC-style treatment of full building footprints rather than a single
  enclosing circle.
\end{itemize}

\section{Levenberg--Marquardt Solver Details}
\label{app:lm}

Given the residual vector $r(p) \in \RR^{n-1}$ of~\eqref{eq:param} and
Jacobian $J = \partial r / \partial p$, Levenberg--Marquardt updates
\begin{equation}
  p^{(t+1)} = p^{(t)}
    - \bigl( J^\top J + \lambda_t\, \mathrm{diag}(J^\top J) \bigr)^{-1}
      J^\top r(p^{(t)}),
  \label{eq:lm}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_t$ is an adaptive damping parameter. When $\lambda_t \to 0$
the update reduces to the Gauss--Newton step (fast second-order convergence
near a good solution); when $\lambda_t \to \infty$ it reduces to a small
gradient-descent step (stable near ill-conditioned regions). The damping is
increased whenever a proposed step fails to reduce $\|r\|_2$ and decreased
on successful steps. For the Boulder County $12$-vertex problem the solver reduces the
LM cost to $\approx 5.4 \times 10^{-2}$ after seven restart passes.
An optimal cyclic rotation of the vertex ordering (roll~$=5$) is applied
before the solve to place the longest polygon side as the outer-arc normalisation
term, keeping all target side-length ratios below unity and reducing the
maximum ratio from $\approx 31$ to $\approx 0.87$. The remaining cost
reflects mild crowding of four free pre-vertices into two sub-intervals
of width $\approx 0.02$ near $\zeta \approx 0.44$ and $\zeta \approx 0.76$.
